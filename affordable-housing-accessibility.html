Project 5 · Journal article

Do residents of Affordable Housing Communities in China suffer from relative accessibility deprivation? A case study of Nanjing

Investigating how the location of affordable housing shapes residents’ access to services and their everyday lives.

Wen Zeng · Philip Rees · Lili Xiang

Cities · 2019

View Published Article
Nanjing
Urban case study
Mixed methods
Spatial analysis and household survey
2019
Cities, volume 90

Research Overview

Affordable housing is frequently built on urban fringes, where land is less costly but services may be difficult to reach. This paper asks whether residents of Affordable Housing Communities in Nanjing experience poorer access to services than residents of Other Housing Communities.

Data & Approach

The study measures distances and estimates walking times between residential land parcels and service facilities. It compares accessibility across housing types and combines the city-wide spatial analysis with a household survey of residents’ daily activities, satisfaction and community attachment.

Key Findings

Relative accessibility deprivation

Affordable Housing Communities have significantly poorer service access than Other Housing Communities in the study.

A shortfall at different service levels

Disadvantage reflects both insufficient neighbourhood provision of lower-order services and poor access to higher-order services.

Consequences for everyday life

The household survey identifies low satisfaction, weak community attachment and a desire to move among affordable housing residents.

Housing location is a policy concern

The findings show why housing affordability should be considered together with access to the facilities and services residents need.

Research & Policy Implications

The study argues that service provision should keep pace with affordable housing construction. Locating and planning housing alongside supporting facilities can help these communities become better places to live, rather than treating the delivery of housing units as the only objective.
