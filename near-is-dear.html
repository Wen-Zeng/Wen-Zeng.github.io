Project 3 · Working paper

Near Is Dear: Remoteness, Soft Information, and Stock Returns

How does the geographic remoteness of a firm’s headquarters shape soft information flows and the pricing of its stock?

Chao Gao · Wen Zeng

Read Paper on SSRN
1996–2020
Main sample period
12,775 firms
Headquartered in the contiguous United States
Soft information
Geography, information frictions and stock returns

Research Overview

Geographic proximity can facilitate the exchange of qualitative information that is difficult to standardize or convey through formal disclosures. This paper asks whether remote headquarters impede the dissemination of such soft information and whether these frictions are reflected in stock prices.

We construct continuous remoteness measures using firm headquarters, county population centers and population data. The framework considers both physical distance and travel time, including ground travel and air connections, to capture access to a broad population.

Key Findings

A persistent remoteness premium

The paper reports that remotely headquartered stocks outperform proximate stocks by 8.59% annually on a risk-adjusted basis. The return pattern persists over longer horizons.

Dispersed operations reduce the effect

The return premium is mitigated among firms with geographically dispersed operations, consistent with operating locations serving as additional information hubs.

Slower incorporation of information

Post-earnings announcement drift and the return predictability of aggregate mutual fund trading are observed among remote firms, highlighting differences in price discovery.

Less soft news, larger reactions

Remote firms have less public soft news. When such news is released, its sentiment is more strongly associated with market reactions for remote firms.

Findings and sample figures refer to the supplied manuscript, available through SSRN abstract 4509746.

Data & Empirical Approach

The main sample covers common stocks listed on the NYSE, Nasdaq and Amex, with 1,191,456 firm-month observations. The analysis combines geographic remoteness with stock returns, firm characteristics, mutual fund trading and public news data. Tests using RavenPack news data begin in 2000.

Alongside portfolio sorts and cross-sectional return regressions, the paper examines changes in accessibility using a difference-in-differences framework. These tests provide evidence on the link between remoteness and the availability of public soft news.

Research Contribution

The study links the geography of firm headquarters to information dissemination, information risk and asset pricing. It also offers an information-frictions perspective on delayed responses to earnings announcements and the informational role of institutional investors.
