Project 2 · Working paper

A Comprehensive GIS Database for China’s Surface Transport Network with Implications for Transport and Socioeconomics Research

Examining how China’s transport network expanded and how infrastructure growth changed access and network connections.

Steven J. Davis · Meijun Qian · Wen Zeng

Read Paper (NBER PDF) Explore the Database Project

Research Overview

The paper introduces the GIS database and uses it to study the evolution of China’s surface transport network from 1993 to 2020. It examines network growth, access to transport entry points, and county-level connectivity and centrality.

A central methodological issue is the distinction between proximity to a motorway route and access through a motorway entrance. The paper shows that these distances can differ substantially and that the differences vary with time, terrain and economic development. This matters for research estimating the effects of transport improvements.

The underlying data are presented separately in the database project; selected layers can also be explored through the interactive map.

Research Highlights

A rapidly expanding network

Total network length grew to more than three times its 1993 level. Motorways and high-speed rail accounted for roughly half of the increase.

Closer access to transport

In the February 2025 paper, average distance from county centroids to transport access points fell from 13.28 km to 5.81 km between 1993 and 2020.

Stronger network connections

The same version reports increases of 22% in county-level connectivity and 115% in centrality.

Actual access matters

Distance to motorway entrances can differ substantially from straight-line distance to routes, with implications for studies of infrastructure’s economic effects.

Source: February 2025 working paper (BFI PDF). Figures refer to that version.
