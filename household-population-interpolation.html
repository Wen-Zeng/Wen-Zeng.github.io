Project 6 · Journal article

Using household counts as ancillary information for areal interpolation of population: Comparing formal and informal, online data sources

Using census household counts and online property data to improve estimates of population across geographic areas.

Wen Zeng · Alexis Comber

Computers, Environment and Urban Systems · 2020

View Published Article
Leeds & Qingdao
Case studies in the UK and China
Household Proportion
Population interpolation method
2020
Volume 80, article 101440

Research Overview

Fine-scale population information is needed for planning and geographic analysis, but population statistics are often available only for larger areas or incompatible boundaries. This paper investigates whether household counts can help redistribute population from source zones to target zones more accurately.

Data & Approach

The proposed Household Proportion (HP) method uses household counts as ancillary information for areal interpolation. The study compares formal census household data with informal residential property information from commercial websites, and evaluates the approach in Leeds, UK, and Qingdao, China. Fine-scale census observations in the Leeds case support validation.

Key Findings

Household data improve population estimates

In the study’s comparisons, the HP method produces lower errors than the other interpolation approaches considered.

Online property data offer useful information

Residential property listings provide a potential alternative source of household-related information where detailed formal data are limited.

Two contrasting urban contexts

The Leeds and Qingdao case studies examine the use of these methods under different data availability and urban conditions.

Coverage and representativeness remain important

Online sources require careful assessment of their spatial coverage, representativeness and timing before they are used to estimate population.

Research & Policy Implications

The work demonstrates an opportunity to incorporate freely accessible, geolocated data into population analysis. It provides a practical basis for finer-scale estimates while emphasizing that the quality and suitability of ancillary data must be examined for each setting.
