Project 5 · Journal article

Evaluation of Life Quality and its Spatial Mismatch with Local Economic Development in Large Chinese Cities

Examining whether urban economic development and quality of life advance together across China’s large cities.

Wen Zeng · Lili Xiang · Wenhe Zhang

Applied Research in Quality of Life · 2020

View Published Article
39 cities
Large Chinese cities
Spatial mismatch
Quality of life and economic development
2020
Journal issue; online publication in 2018

Research Overview

Economic growth is often treated as a sign of urban progress, but it does not necessarily translate into better living conditions. This study develops a quality-of-life assessment for 39 large Chinese cities and compares the spatial distribution of life quality with local economic development.

Data & Approach

The analysis constructs a composite quality-of-life index and applies a spatial mismatch approach to compare cities’ performance. It examines both differences between cities and how the relationship between life quality and economic development varies geographically.

Key Findings

Uneven urban life quality

Quality of life differs substantially among the cities studied. Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Beijing, Ningbo and Shanghai rank highest in the study’s assessment.

Growth and well-being do not fully align

The spatial pattern of quality of life does not simply reproduce the pattern of local economic development. Different cities exhibit different forms of mismatch.

A distinctive pattern in western cities

In large cities across western China, quality-of-life performance is generally stronger relative to their local economic development performance.

Economic structure matters

The relationship involves more than the scale of the economy. The study emphasizes the importance of economic structure and the types of industries supporting urban development.

Research & Policy Implications

The findings support an urban development agenda that evaluates living conditions alongside economic outcomes. The paper recommends attention to high value-added, environmentally friendly industries that can support both local economic development and quality of life.
